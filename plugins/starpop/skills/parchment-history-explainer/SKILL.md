# Parchment explainer

Create a full-length vertical story ad built from rapidly cut parchment illustrations, six numbered chapters, continuous male narration, and a late product reveal. It wins by making a complicated problem visually simple, sustaining curiosity through an origin-and-displacement story, then presenting the product as the practical resolution.

Use Starpop’s standard voiceover-first timing, storyboard, generation, and editing rules; the instructions below add only this format’s requirements.

## Format rules

- Default to 9:16, approximately three minutes, delivered at 1080 × 1920 and 30 fps. A natural approved narration around 3:10–3:35 is compatible with this format. Do not silently shorten it to a conventional 30- or 60-second spot.
- Build approximately 100–115 phrase-level shots for a full-length version. Typical holds last 1–2 seconds; brief ingredient beats can be shorter, while complex illustrations may hold for 3–5 seconds. Follow spoken meaning rather than enforcing equal durations.
- Use hard cuts between full-frame illustrations. The finished treatment is a still-image story, not an animated cartoon: no blinking, lip movement, galloping, wind, morphing, or invented parallax. Restrained camera pans are optional, not necessary for completion.
- Keep a cohesive warm aged-paper world: cream parchment, worn edges, brown ink contours, muted ochre, rust, green and dusty blue, softly shaded storybook characters, botanical specimens, rustic interiors, and miniature workers inside mechanical cutaways. Avoid photoreal scenes, glossy 3D animation, and unrelated illustration styles.
- Translate abstract ideas into concrete tableaux: pipes feeding a body, an organ as a warehouse, tiny workers operating bellows or valves, boats carrying sacks, an eraser removing drawings, a balance scale comparing value. Use metaphors to explain approved claims, not to manufacture scientific proof.
- Keep the first product reveal late, around 80% into the story. Make the final section visually dense with the actual package, ingredients, dosage form, substantiated proof, benefits, value, offer, and CTA.
- Add short, bold white captions with a dark outline near the bottom. Keep them separate from generated art, synchronized to the actual recording, and clear of packaging and important diagrams.

## 1. Set the adaptation

**Goal:** Establish what stays and what must change without a long intake.

Ask only for missing essentials:

1. **Product and appearance:** name, a clear packshot, and actual ingredients or components and dosage form. Default to the supplied product information; request a packshot if none exists.
2. **Promise and close:** approved benefits, supporting facts, offer, guarantee, and CTA or destination. Default to verified product facts and a simple product CTA; omit unknown discounts, customer counts, certifications, deadlines, and guarantees.
3. **Replication preference:** if a reference exists, ask what to keep versus replace and whether to match closely or take inspiration. Default to this format’s look, six-step structure, and full length, with product-specific copy adapted for the new product.

Do not ask for an actor: this is off-camera narration over illustrations. Do not assume a new formula inherits another product’s claims. Preserve a historical or mechanism narrative only when it remains accurate and relevant; otherwise keep its storytelling function and rewrite its substance.

**Hand-off:** Summarize the retained format, necessary substitutions, and full-length target, then proceed to the complete script.

## 2. Approve the full story

**Goal:** Resolve the entire narrative before buying production assets.

Use this six-step spine:

| Beat | Story function | Visual vocabulary |
|---|---|---|
| Hook, first 4–5 seconds | Name a surprising explanation for the audience’s problem; promise simple understanding | One striking system-to-person metaphor, followed by a childlike explanation tableau |
| Step 1 | Establish the useful older practice or origin story | Landscapes, historical everyday life, specimens, early mechanism metaphor |
| Step 2 | Explain how that practice was displaced or forgotten | Erasers, changing shops, books, shelves, institutional transitions |
| Step 3 | Expose a misconception or inadequate substitute | Contrasting labels and outcomes, literal visual jokes |
| Step 4 | Connect the present-day cause to recognizable frustration | Mechanical cutaways, accumulation metaphors, clothing or routine discomfort |
| Step 5 | Explain why the better approach remains overlooked | Cost, convenience, manufacturing, or other substantiated tradeoffs |
| Step 6 and close | Introduce the brand as the resolution | Ingredient inspection, package reveal, actual dosage form, evidence, benefits, value and offer |

Spend most of the runtime teaching the story; reserve roughly the last fifth to quarter for Step 6 and the selling sequence. Use numbered chapter cards with the same raised ochre button on parchment. Let timed captions supply each step number; add any larger card lettering separately in the edit.

Use short declarative sentences, concrete nouns, emphatic contrasts, and continuous narration across shot boundaries. Example hook pattern, not fixed copy: “How [a surprising change] affected [everyday problem], explained like you’re five.”

Show the full script and a concise substitution list. Check historical accuracy, mechanism claims, dosage, manufacturing claims, social proof, and commercial terms. Do not default to the source’s medical accusations, treatment claims, or urgency. Remove transcription errors and false starts before recording.

**Hand-off:** Get approval for the complete script, voice direction, and narration generation.

## 3. Lock the narration

**Goal:** Establish a natural, intelligible master recording that will not change halfway through production.

Use **ElevenLabs Eleven v4** for the voiceover. The selected voice was **Grant — Deep, Expressive British**, reference `voice:elevenlabs:cnq6HZP9Mrjsf97pVK6b`. Default to this warm, deep, clear male storyteller: serious documentary confidence, crisp articulation, brisk emphatic delivery, no singing and no on-camera performance.

Measure and listen to the entire take. Check pronunciation, malformed phrases, pauses, continuity, and actual runtime. If it runs longer than the reference, offer a natural retake or acceptance of the longer version; do not accelerate the audio afterward merely to hit the reference duration.

Once the user accepts a take, lock it. The original user chose a 3:33 recording over a shorter retake; that teaches respect for an approved recording, not a default preference for errors or false starts. Request approval before any paid retake.

**Hand-off:** Confirm the exact accepted runtime and use that recording for all remaining timing.

## 4. Prove the illustrated treatment

**Goal:** Validate the style, captions, pacing, and package translation before producing the full asset set.

Build an opening preview of approximately 25–30 seconds, usually about 15 shots. Use **Nano Banana 2.1** for illustration creation and reference-guided cleanup or substitutions.

For a close adaptation with usable source frames, preserve composition, identities, clothing, poses, object counts, pipe routes, perspective, and lighting. Remove old subtitles and restore the artwork beneath them rather than redesigning the scene. For an original adaptation, reproduce the same visual grammar with new, relevant tableaux.

Keep captions and chapter lettering out of generated artwork. Preserve meaningful genuine package printing and historical text only where relevant; do not indiscriminately erase labels that the story needs.

For every product replacement, separate reference responsibilities:

- Take package silhouette, closure, colors, logo, and genuine label from the user’s packshot.
- Take rendering style, framing, characters, environment, and light from the illustration reference.
- Exclude the packshot’s photographic background and exclude the previous product’s bottle shape and label.

Inspect the stills before assembly. Use direct image holds by default. Do not spend video-generation credits to recreate frozen illustrations; failed motion attempts must not block an otherwise faithful still-image ad. Any optional paid motion test needs its own approval.

**Hand-off:** Obtain approval for the opening still batch before generation, then show the assembled preview and ask to continue with the same treatment for the remaining chapters.

## 5. Complete the remaining chapters

**Goal:** Produce every remaining story and selling beat without losing consistency.

Treat approval to finish the agreed plan as authorization for its remaining still batches; do not repeatedly reopen settled creative decisions. Keep an asset ledger by shot and chapter so interrupted work resumes accurately.

Apply substitutions everywhere, not just in the hero reveal: packages on conveyors, shelves, trays, scales, crates, and in people’s hands must all match the new product. Match the actual number of ingredients and the actual dosage form. A seamless liquid softgel must not become a two-piece powder capsule. Adjust hand contact only as necessary to fit the replacement package.

Keep historical specimens in historical scenes when the script calls for them; replace ingredient demonstrations in the product section with the product’s real ingredients. Do not invent manufacturing methods or dosing instructions to fill a visual slot.

Use **V6** for an approved instrumental music bed. Favor quiet ambient electronic synth pads, a restrained mid-tempo pulse, and low-to-moderate energy. Gentle plucked textures are acceptable; avoid a dominant folk melody, vocals, dramatic drops, or effects that compete with speech. The delivered version used the gentler, more electronic candidate quietly rather than achieving the source’s more dramatic score.

Get approval for music generation. Check the actual result and length, and cover the whole ad with an unobtrusive loop, extension, or chapter arrangement. A short preview track is not automatically a complete full-length bed.

If an asset is recovering, keep it marked pending. Do not launch a duplicate paid attempt automatically or substitute unrelated media. Resume when it becomes available and continue through assembly.

**Hand-off:** Confirm that every required shot, including product, offer, and ending, is available and inspected; proceed directly to the final edit.

## 6. Assemble, render, and check

**Goal:** Deliver the full advertisement, not just its assets or opening chapter.

Preserve the approved master narration at natural speed. Assemble the full phrase-level cut sequence, add fresh synchronized captions, and keep music clearly below speech. Chapter assemblies are acceptable for managing the long sequence, but their joins must preserve timing and avoid doubled narration or music.

An intentional brief black interstitial can punctuate the story’s reversal, provided its spoken words remain visible as captions. Treat expected still holds and intentional black as creative choices, not reasons to add animation. Distinguish them from missing footage.

Check caption readability, step numbering, package accuracy, ingredient count, all offer terms, chapter joins, full music coverage, and the final spoken sentence. Fix fast caption chunks by regrouping phrases rather than changing an approved voice take. Aim near −14 LUFS with true peaks below −1 dBTP while preserving intelligibility.

Completion requires the entire approved runtime, every chapter, the product reveal, close, and a rendered export with no accidental gaps or clipped ending. Once assets are ready, carry the work through the render and final inspection without stopping at a progress report. Deliver the full video and briefly disclose any remaining material deviations.

**Hand-off:** Present the finished full-length export with runtime, dimensions, and only meaningful check notes.

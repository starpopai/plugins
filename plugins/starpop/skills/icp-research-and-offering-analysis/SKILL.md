# ICP Research and Offering Analysis

Produce current, evidence-backed research for brand strategy and ad creative.

Keep customer voice, brand claims, category facts, and strategic inference distinct.

## Scope and evidence

Ground research in the intended brand and exact product. Reuse sufficient context already available, and resolve supplied URLs to the intended offering.

Use current primary sources for brand, competitor, category, policy, and industry facts. Use customer accounts for lived experiences, purchase triggers, failed alternatives, objections, and authentic language. Never invent evidence.

This skill produces research and strategy.

Do not generate images, videos, speech, or music unless the user separately asks to create media after reviewing the research.

## Establish the brief

Identify the focal brand and canonical website, product or category, market, known competitors, initial hypotheses, target channels and funnel stages, and any excluded sources.

Infer non-critical gaps from workspace and website evidence and label the assumptions.

Ask one concise question only when the focal brand or product cannot be identified reliably, or when the answer would materially change the scope.

Record the research date, market, currency, and relevant variants.

Treat prices, promotions, stock, shipping, trials, returns, and warranties as time-sensitive.

## Match evidence to the claim

| Source                                                                | Appropriate use                                                        | Do not use it to prove                              |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Customer discussions, forums, independent reviews, and video comments | Lived experience, wording, pain, struggle, triggers, and objections    | Product-wide rates, universal outcomes, or efficacy |
| Primary industry or regulatory sources                                | Standards, definitions, safety, and category facts                     | Individual customer sentiment                       |
| Focal brand website and workspace product data                        | Current features, terms, prices, policies, and stated claims           | Independent effectiveness or customer satisfaction  |
| Competitor website and ads                                            | The competitor's stated position and offer                             | Independent proof that its promise works            |
| Retailer or marketplace reviews                                       | Buyer language and product-specific experiences, with platform context | Category prevalence or authoritative brand policy   |

Prefer original customer posts to affiliate pages, scraped aggregators, summaries, and marketing copy.

Prefer original sources over pages quoting them.

For every piece of evidence:

1. Keep the quote or paraphrase adjacent to its direct source URL.

2. Quote only exact wording present in the source evidence. Never reconstruct or strengthen a quote from memory or a summary.

3. Identify whether text came from a post, result excerpt, or top comment. Label a parent-post URL used for a returned top comment.

4. Preserve the speaker's meaning and relevant product, variant, community, or platform context.

5. Label on-site testimonials, incentivized reviews, sponsored content, and unverifiable identities.

6. Do not infer prevalence from anecdotes. Say "recurring in the sampled sources," not "most customers," unless representative data supports it.

7. Remove sources the user distrusts and replace affected evidence where possible.

Never fabricate a quote, URL, date, review status, statistic, or timestamp.

If the available evidence does not expose exact wording, paraphrase and cite it instead of presenting it as a quotation.

## Research workflow

### 1. Ground in the focal brand and product

Establish the active brand and its relevant offerings.

For product-specific requests, identify the exact product and obtain its detailed facts. Gather images or recent creatives only when relevant.

Treat workspace data as the starting point, not complete proof.

Use live search to verify current public features, policies, pricing, promotions, and claims against the canonical brand domain.

### 2. Map the competitor landscape

Classify alternatives as direct specialists, category generalists, budget or do-it-yourself alternatives, premium or niche alternatives, and the status quo or "do nothing" option.

Find competitors from customer comparisons, category searches, retailer listings, and the user's input.

Do not assume search rank equals market importance.

### 3. Research customer problems and purchase triggers

Run several focused searches in parallel when possible.

Adapt communities and terms to the category.

Cover:

- Category problems, complaints, discomfort, and frustration

- Competitor comparisons and switching stories

- Lifestyle-specific upgrade problems

- "Finally replaced," "gave up on," "why I switched," and other purchase triggers

- Competitor problems, disappointment, and returns

- "What I wish I knew," favorite features, setup, sizing, maintenance, shipping, and returns

Keep customer testimony distinct from broader market research and primary-source facts.

Capture confirming and disconfirming evidence for initial hypotheses.

For each theme, record customer situation and segment, trigger or job to be done, pain point, struggle, prior alternatives, objection, desired outcome, emotional intensity, and evidence with direct URLs.

Aim for three independent customer sources per major angle.

When fewer exist, show the evidence count and lower confidence rather than filling the quota with weak material.

### 4. Analyze competitor positioning

For each relevant competitor, distinguish:

- Stated position: the promise or differentiator on its official site

- Observed friction: customer-reported complaints or tradeoffs

- Opportunity: an evidence-grounded position the focal brand could credibly own

Do not convert a complaint about one model, order, region, or period into a company-wide weakness.

Note contradictory experiences and sample limitations.

### 5. Audit the focal brand's offer

Research product, collection, FAQ, shipping, returns, warranty, about, and certification pages on the canonical domain.

Record every material claim used in the answer:

| Claim   | Status                                         | Scope or conditions                    | Evidence                                  | Source       | Checked |
| ------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------ | ------- |
| [Claim] | Verified / Partial / Conflicting / Unsupported | [Variant, region, exception, end date] | [Tight paraphrase or short exact excerpt] | [Direct URL] | [Date]  |

Use Verified only when workspace data or an official page directly supports the wording.

Use Partial when the statement is broader than its source.

Use Conflicting when authoritative sources disagree, and cite each one.

Use Unsupported when no authoritative source was found.

Treat quiz results, cart offers, geographic rules, custom-order exceptions, and time-limited promotions as conditional.

Never generalize them into universal policies.

### 6. Develop ad angles

Each angle must include:

- Short, memorable name

- First-person ICP problem statement synthesized from evidence; do not put it in quotation marks unless it is verbatim

- Segment and purchase trigger

- Three to six short customer quotes when available, each paired directly with its URL

- Concise emotional hook

- Funnel stage: TOF, MOF, or BOF

- Confidence: high, medium, or low, based on relevance, independence, recency, and consistency

- Brand proof required before the angle can be used as a product claim

Cover new category entrants, replacement or upgrade buyers, lifestyle-specific segments, problem-aware and solution-unaware buyers, solution-aware and brand-unaware buyers, and failed-solution escalators when supported.

Target 8–12 genuinely distinct angles.

Return fewer when the evidence does not support more and explain the gap instead of relabelling the same pain.

### 7. Build the offering chain

Start each row from a verified feature or policy:

| Element    | Development rule                                                                                     |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| FEATURE    | State the verified product or service attribute and link its official source.                        |
| BENEFIT    | Translate what the feature enables; label it as an inference unless the source states it.            |
| DESIRE     | Describe the emotionally meaningful outcome supported by customer evidence.                          |
| CORE NEED  | Name the underlying need, such as security, comfort, control, belonging, esteem, or self-expression. |
| PAIN POINT | Use customer language and link the source.                                                           |
| STRUGGLE   | Describe prior attempts, costs, friction, or compromises supported by evidence.                      |
| OBJECTION  | State why the buyer may delay, distrust, or reject the offer.                                        |
| HANDLING   | Use only verified proof, policy, process, demonstration, or risk reversal; link every factual claim. |

Never invent objection handling.

When the brand lacks proof, write Proof gap and recommend the asset, test, clarification, or policy needed.

## Deliverables

Lead with a concise executive summary, research scope, date, limitations, and highest-confidence opportunities.

Then provide:

### ICP angles

| #   | Angle | Segment / trigger | ICP problem statement | Evidence quotes and direct links | Emotional hook | Funnel | Confidence | Brand proof required |
| --- | ----- | ----------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------- | -------------- | ------ | ---------- | -------------------- |

Keep each quote adjacent to its link inside the same cell.

### Funnel mapping

| Funnel stage | Recommended angles | Rationale                                              |
| ------------ | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| TOF          | [Angle numbers]    | [Problem recognition or identity relevance]            |
| MOF          | [Angle numbers]    | [Category education or differentiation]                |
| BOF          | [Angle numbers]    | [Verified proof, objection handling, or risk reversal] |

### Competitive positioning

| Competitor / type | Stated position | Observed friction | Opportunity for focal brand | Evidence and direct links | Confidence / caveat |
| ----------------- | --------------- | ----------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------- |

### Offering breakdown

| #   | FEATURE | BENEFIT | DESIRE | CORE NEED | PAIN POINT | STRUGGLE | OBJECTION | HANDLING |
| --- | ------- | ------- | ------ | --------- | ---------- | -------- | --------- | -------- |

Link official feature evidence in FEATURE, customer evidence in PAIN POINT or STRUGGLE, and verification sources in HANDLING.

### Claim verification ledger

Include the ledger for every factual focal-brand claim used in the deliverables.

Omit unrelated claims found during browsing.

## Final quality gate

- Every exact quote is present in returned research evidence and paired with its source URL.

- Customer posts, result excerpts, top comments, official claims, and strategic inferences remain distinct.

- Every focal-brand feature, price, policy, process, statistic, certification, and objection-handling claim has an authoritative source or a visible non-verified status.

- Conditional and time-limited claims show their scope and check date.

- Conflicts and unsupported claims remain visible rather than silently resolved.

- Anecdotes are not presented as prevalence data.

- Multiple segments, buyer stages, and failed alternatives are represented where evidence permits.

- Excluded sources have been removed from affected rows.

When the user challenges a claim or source, rerun targeted research, replace or downgrade affected evidence, update every affected deliverable, and summarize what changed and why.
